# Drawing the mantling

The scrolling material that descends from the helm in these achievements is generally called *mantling* or *lambrequin*. The last supplied image shows a different part of the achievement, a winged crest above a barred helm. In this experiment the helm establishes the point of attachment. The generative work concerns the red cloth and its white lining.

## Historical evidence

W. H. St John Hope describes a cloth hanging from the helm and shows its edges cut into dags. His illustrations offer two dated witnesses to the disposition of the cloth around the helmet: the seal of William, Lord Hastings, about 1461 (fig. 70), and that of William de la Pole, Earl of Suffolk, 1415 (fig. 71). Hope records red mantling lined with white or ermine alongside examples using other colours. For our purposes the distinction between the *two surfaces* matters more than assigning them any particular tinctures. [Hope, *Heraldry for Craftsmen & Designers*, chapter V, “Mantlings,” pp. 139–40](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/45181/45181-h/45181-h.htm).

A. C. Fox-Davies locates the ornament at the helm, descending along the shield. He identifies the treatment of the mantling as an area of substantial artistic discretion and records exceptional cases in which the crest continues into the mantling. The prototype consequently treats the shape of a return or the spacing of dags as attributes of an emblazonment. Mirroring is an editorial aid requested for this experiment. [Fox-Davies, *A Complete Guide to Heraldry*, chapter XXIV, “The Mantling or Lambrequin”](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/A_Complete_Guide_to_Heraldry/Chapter_24).

Albrecht Dürer’s *Coat of Arms with the Cock*, circa 1503, offers a particularly good comparison for vine-like mantling. The University of Michigan Museum of Art identifies the rooster's feathers, the metal helm and the surrounding acanthus as three distinct textures within the engraving. That distinction informs the Acanthus method. Its incident belongs to an articulated edge and a central fold, while the engraving marks are a separate choice of finish. [UMMA, accession 1960/2.34](https://umma.umich.edu/objects/coat-of-arms-with-the-cock-1960-2-34/).

## Three constructions

**Acanthus.** Two cut contours flank a smoothed guide. Raised lobes have a convex shoulder, a recurving point and a deep re-entrant space. The central rib carries the eye across irregularities of silhouette.

**Slashed cloth.** A broad strip develops long pointed cuts and subordinate forked tongues. The face and lining exchange at turns. Try changing the same guide from this method to Acanthus: the long uninterrupted spans of cloth should survive at a scale where the leaves read as a succession of smaller masses.

**Scrollwork.** Narrower bands send out independently coiling shoots. Useful detail comes from the change in scale between a main return and its small offshoots. An enclosed eye is an intended opening in the composition.

## How to compare the samples

The three horizontal bands of Comparison.png are *Canopy*, *Cascade* and *Volutes*. Each band uses identical guide data across the three methods. Canopy projects laterally from the helm, Cascade descends to the shield point, and Volutes leaves broad spaces between the upper and lower returns. Canopy uses flat colour, contours and ribs. Cascade adds hatching. Volutes uses shading in place of hatching. A direct comparison of finishes is available by holding one composition and method fixed and toggling individual layers in the page.

The guide stores the direction and length of each gesture, local face reversals, terminal curl, scale and initial face. The engine constructs, ornaments and paints the right half before reflecting the result about the shield axis. Further hand-drawn studies should test whether an opening remains legible at phone size, whether the red and white read as two faces of a continuous material, and whether the attachment behind the helm remains clear as branches are added.

## Revision after visual review

The first Canopy study gave its descending arms a weak attachment at each shoulder. The revised guides carry the red cloth outward from behind the helm before exposing the broad white lining and place the smaller lower branch beneath its parent. On reviewing the new proof the independent critic could follow the drape continuously from helm to curled end, with the open interval beside the shield preserved.
